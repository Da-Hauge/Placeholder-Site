1. Controller

The controller under Art. 4(7) GDPR, together with the full postal address and telephone number required by German law, is named in the Impressum. Contact by email: maximilian@haug-it.eu.

No data protection officer has been appointed. On the current assessment none is required under § 38 BDSG — that assessment is part of the legal review.

2. What this site does not do

  • No cookies.
  • No analytics, tracking or A/B testing.
  • No fingerprinting.
  • No external fonts, icon services or JavaScript libraries — no CDN.
  • No embedded maps, videos, social plugins or captchas.
  • No contact form and no newsletter sign-up.
  • No sessionStorage, no IndexedDB.
  • Nothing on this site is transmitted to a third party as a result of you reading it.

3. What is stored on your device

Two entries, both in localStorage, both written only when you actually operate the relevant control. Nothing is stored if you just read the page.

Entries stored in your browser
Key Value Written when Purpose Lifetime
haugit.lang en or de You use the EN/DE switch Remembers the language you chose Until you clear site data
haugit.theme light or dark You use the theme toggle Remembers the colour scheme you chose Until you clear site data
haugit.mcu.progress JSON: watched title IDs and display options You tick a "watched" checkbox or an option toggle in the MCU Tracker Remembers your watch progress Until you clear site data or use "reset progress"
haugit.mcu.tmdbKey Your own, self-supplied TMDb API key You voluntarily paste your own TMDb key into the MCU Tracker Enables optional live poster/rating sync Until you use "clear key" or clear site data

All four entries stay in your browser. They are never transmitted to the server or to the operator, contain no identifier, and allow no conclusion about who you are. The haugit.mcu.tmdbKey entry is the one exception once you fill it in yourself: from then on your browser sends your IP address and the key directly to TMDb on every request inside the MCU Tracker — see section 5 below and docs/verarbeitung.md (V6). Setting the theme back to auto deletes the haugit.theme entry rather than storing the word "auto". You can remove any of these at any time through your browser's site data; the site then works exactly as before.

On your first visit the site reads your browser language and your operating system colour scheme in order to pick a starting point. Reading them is not storage and leaves nothing behind.

Remembering a language and a display preference that you set yourself is strictly necessary in order to provide the service you explicitly requested, which is the exception in § 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG (the German implementation of the ePrivacy Directive). No consent is required, so there is deliberately no cookie banner.

LEGAL-REVIEW: treating a language and theme preference as strictly necessary is the common reading, but it is an assessment rather than settled case law. The zero-storage alternative is to drop both features and let the browser decide on every visit.

4. Visiting the site (server logs)

The site is served by GitHub Pages. As with any web server, technically necessary connection data is processed by the host — in particular your IP address, the time of the request, the URL requested, the amount of data transferred, the referrer and your browser identification. The operator has no direct control over the scope or retention of these logs.

5. Processors and transfers outside the EU

Hosting: GitHub, Inc. (GitHub Pages), 88 Colin P Kelly Jr Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA. GitHub is a US company and delivery may run through servers outside the EU/EEA. That is a third-country transfer within the meaning of Art. 44 ff. GDPR.

Basis of the transfer

GitHub, Inc. has self-certified to the U.S. Department of Commerce that it adheres to the principles of the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework, and additionally relies on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914) for transfers out of the EU, the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland. Both follow from the GitHub General Privacy Statement and the GitHub Data Protection Agreement; checked on 8 September 2026.

GitHub does not publish a concrete retention period for GitHub Pages access logs. Its privacy statement only says in general terms that retention depends on the purpose of collection and on legal obligations.

LEGAL-REVIEW — after that research, exactly three questions remain that need a legal assessment:
  1. Is GitHub a processor under Art. 28 GDPR for the access logs at all, or an independent controller in that respect? Whether a processing agreement is needed depends on the answer.
  2. If one is needed: does the GitHub Data Protection Agreement reach a free personal account? Its scope reads "all Online Services", but GitHub presents the DPA per product for Enterprise Cloud, Teams and Copilot.
  3. Are the DPF certification and the Standard Contractual Clauses sufficient here — and is the certification still active at the time of publication? The list at dataprivacyframework.gov/list has to be checked on the day, because a certification can lapse.
The alternative that disposes of all three: hosting inside the EU/EEA.

TMDB (The Movie Database) — only if you turn it on in the MCU Tracker

The MCU Tracker loads its base data from this site's own source code — no third-party connection happens by default. If you voluntarily paste your own, self-obtained TMDb API key into the tracker's Settings dialog, your browser then sends your IP address, standard HTTP headers and that key directly to api.themoviedb.org and image.tmdb.org — servers operated by TMDB, a US company — on every request inside the tracker. The legal basis is your consent, given by the active act of entering the key (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR); the Settings dialog explains this before you do.

LEGAL-REVIEW: TMDB's own privacy policy names server locations outside the EU/EEA without naming a specific transfer mechanism (DPF certification, Standard Contractual Clauses). Details and the state of this check are in docs/auftragsverarbeiter.md.

7. Your rights

You have the following rights against the controller:

  • Access to the data processed about you (Art. 15 GDPR)
  • Rectification of inaccurate data (Art. 16 GDPR)
  • Erasure (Art. 17 GDPR)
  • Restriction of processing (Art. 18 GDPR)
  • Data portability (Art. 20 GDPR)
  • Objection to processing based on Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (Art. 21 GDPR)
  • Complaint to a supervisory authority (Art. 77 GDPR)
